What is an MMI?

MMI stands for multiple mini interview. You move through a series of short stations, each with a new interviewer and a new task. One bad station does not sink you, because every station is marked separately.

Most UK medical schools use one: 41 of the 51 schools in our medical school comparison run an MMI. Timings and station types differ between schools, so check each school’s format before you practise.

How to approach every station type

Learn one simple structure per station type. It gives you something to hold on to when your mind goes blank.

Station type

Motivation for medicine

  • Lead with a specific moment, not “I want to help people”
  • Show you know the hard parts: shifts, uncertainty, lifelong exams
  • Say why a doctor, not a nurse, paramedic or physio
  • End with what you have done to test that decision
Station type

Ethics

  • Name the tension first: whose interests pull against each other?
  • Use the four principles: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice
  • Give both sides fairly before you decide
  • Land on a clear, safe answer and say what you would check next
Station type

Role play

  • Introduce yourself and why you are there
  • Ask open questions, then explore their ideas, concerns and expectations
  • Name the emotion you see: “You seem really worried about this”
  • Summarise and agree a next step before time runs out
Station type

Situational judgement

  • Ask: is anyone at risk right now?
  • Stay within your role and be honest about what you do not know
  • Speak to the person involved before going over their head
  • Escalate to someone senior when safety or honesty is at stake
Station type

Data and problem solving

  • Read the title, axes and units before the numbers
  • Say what the data shows, then what it cannot show
  • Correlation is not cause: name other explanations
  • Say what extra information you would want next
Station type

Teamwork and group tasks

  • Agree the goal and a rough plan in the first minutes
  • Build on others by name: “Adding to Sam’s point…”
  • Invite quieter people in with a specific question
  • Keep time and summarise decisions out loud

A 4-week MMI practice plan

Four weeks is enough to go from nervous to ready if you practise out loud, not just in your head.

  1. Week 1

    Know the format and your stories

    • List each school’s MMI format and timings
    • Write six stories: motivation, teamwork, leadership, a mistake, a challenge, caring for someone
    • Learn the six NHS values and the four ethical principles
  2. Week 2

    Practise with structure

    • One station type a day, untimed, using the structures above
    • Record yourself on your phone and watch it back once
    • Read two health news stories a week and form a view
  3. Week 3

    Add the timer

    • Do stations at real length with a friend or tutor
    • Get one piece of feedback per station and fix only that
    • Practise the reset: breathe, re-read, start fresh
  4. Week 4

    Full mock circuits

    • Run full back-to-back circuits
    • Spend extra time on your weakest station type
    • Plan the day: route, clothes, sleep, food

What to read around

Interviewers do not expect expert knowledge. They want to see that you follow what is happening in healthcare and can discuss it sensibly.

How doctors are expected to behave

  • The GMC’s Good medical practice
  • The six NHS values
  • Confidentiality, consent and Gillick competence

How to read the news well

  • Pick one story a week and summarise it in two sentences
  • Ask: who is affected, and what are the trade-offs?
  • Form a balanced view you could defend for two minutes

Common MMI mistakes

  • Memorising scripts→Prepare ideas and examples, then speak naturally
  • Rushing to an answer→Spend ten seconds planning your structure
  • Only saying what you did→Say what you learnt from it
  • Arguing one side of an ethics case→Show both sides, then decide
  • Ignoring the actor’s feelings in role play→Name the emotion and respond to it
  • Carrying a bad station into the next→Reset. Read. Think. Start again
