What is an MMI?

MMI stands for multiple mini interview. Instead of one long panel interview, you move through a series of short stations. Each station has a new interviewer and a new task.

Most UK medical schools now use one: 41 of the 51 schools in our medical school comparison run an MMI. Timings and station types vary from school to school, so your practice should match the schools you applied to.

Stations we practise

01

Motivation for medicine

Why medicine, and why you. We help you prepare ideas and examples, not scripts.

02

Ethics

Weigh up a dilemma out loud, explain your reasoning and reach a sensible answer.

03

Role play

Talk to someone playing a patient or colleague. Listen, clarify, then respond.

04

Situational judgement

What would you do, and why? Keep it safe, realistic and honest about your role.

05

Data and problem solving

Read the information, find the problem and explain your next step clearly.

06

Teamwork

Some schools include group tasks. We practise listening, inviting others in and summarising.
