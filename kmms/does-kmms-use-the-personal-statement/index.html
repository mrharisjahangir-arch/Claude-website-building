Does KMMS use the personal statement?

Yes, KMMS uses the personal statement, but how much it matters depends on your applicant group. Here is what Kent and Medway Medical School says, and how to write one that works for KMMS and your other choices.

Written by Haris, KMMS medical student · Updated September 2026

Get personal statement help →Back to the KMMS guide

Short answer

Yes, but it depends on your applicant group.

For standard Groups A and B, KMMS focuses on your grades in the context of your school (see KMMS contextual admissions). For applicants where reliable school data cannot be used, KMMS says the personal statement and reference are important.

What KMMS says

For standard Groups A and B, KMMS describes Stage 2b as academic contextualisation. It does not publish a general personal statement weighting for these applicants.

For applicants where reliable school data cannot be used, KMMS says the personal statement and reference are important.

Either way, write it properly. The other schools on your UCAS form read it too, and some use it at interview.

The three UCAS questions

  • Why do you want to study this course or subject?
  • How have your qualifications and studies helped you to prepare for this course or subject?
  • What else have you done to prepare outside of education, and why are these experiences useful?

Writing with KMMS in mind

Show the qualities KMMS lists

  • Compassion, communication and teamwork
  • Resilience and responsibility
  • A realistic commitment to Medicine

Reflect, do not list

  • What you did
  • What you noticed
  • What you learnt and changed

Keep it interview-ready

  • Only include what you can discuss for two minutes
  • Know the story behind every line
  • Be ready for “what did you learn?” in your KMMS MMI

How to make it stronger

  1. 1Pick a real experience
  2. 2Say what you noticed
  3. 3Explain what you learnt
  4. 4Keep it honest and specific
