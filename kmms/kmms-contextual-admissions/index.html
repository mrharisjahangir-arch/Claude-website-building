KMMS contextual admissions explained

KMMS contextual admissions compare your grades with how students at your school usually perform. Kent and Medway Medical School calls this Contextualise Everyone. Here is what it means for you.

KMMS MMI mock interview →Back to the KMMS guide

Short answer

KMMS asks: how well did you do in your school?

For many applicants, KMMS compares your results with the average performance of your school. This is called relative attainment. KMMS does not simply pick students with the highest grades or UCAT scores.

Two students, two schools

KMMS looks at how well you performed compared with your school. In simple terms:

Student A

Gets very high grades at a school where very high grades are normal.

School averageStudent A

Student B

Gets slightly lower grades at a school where average results are much lower.

School averageStudent B

KMMS may view Student B as performing more strongly relative to their school. This is called relative attainment.

What it means for you

At a school where very high grades are normal, very high grades are expected. At a school where average results are lower, slightly lower grades can still look strong.

KMMS contextualises GCSEs for relevant applicants, and predicted grades are not used. See Does KMMS use predicted grades?
