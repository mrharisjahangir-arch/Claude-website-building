Does KMMS use predicted grades?

No. KMMS does not use predicted grades in selection. Here is what Kent and Medway Medical School looks at instead, and what that means for your application.

Written by Haris, KMMS medical student · Updated September 2026

KMMS MMI mock interview →Back to the KMMS guide

Short answer

No. KMMS says predicted grades are not used in selection.

For a normal Year 13 applicant, achieved GCSE results are used during contextual selection. Predicted A*A*A* does not automatically make you stronger than another applicant.

What KMMS looks at instead

For many applicants, KMMS compares your grades with the average performance of your school. It calls this Contextualise Everyone. Read KMMS contextual admissions explained.

So your achieved GCSEs matter, alongside passing the UCAT threshold and, if you are shortlisted, the KMMS MMI.

What this means for you

Strong GCSEs

KMMS may suit you well

  • Your achieved results carry weight
  • Compare them with your school’s usual results
  • Then focus on UCAT and the MMI
Strong predictions

Do not rely on them here

  • Predictions help at other schools, not KMMS
  • Balance your four choices
  • Check each school in our medical school finder
Still in Year 11

GCSEs matter for medicine

  • Aim for 9 to 6 across the required subjects
  • English Language, Maths and the sciences count
