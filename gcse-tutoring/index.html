What the science exams really test

Maths in science

Maths marks hide in every paper

  • At least 10% of Biology marks need maths skills
  • At least 20% of Chemistry marks
  • At least 30% of Physics marks
  • Practise rearranging equations and unit conversions early
Practicals

Required practicals come up

  • Know each method step by step
  • Name the independent, dependent and control variables
  • Explain how to make results more accurate
  • Sketch and read results tables and graphs
Command words

Read the question twice

  • State: a short fact, no explanation
  • Describe: what happens
  • Explain: why it happens
  • Compare: similarities and differences, both sides

A GCSE revision timeline

  1. Year 10

    Build good habits

    • Make flashcards as you learn each topic
    • Fix gaps as they appear, not in Year 11
    • Start topic questions from past papers
  2. Year 11 autumn

    Cover and consolidate

    • Finish any unclear topics
    • Past paper questions by topic
    • Learn the required practicals
  3. After mocks

    Target weak areas

    • Go through every lost mark
    • Timed half papers
    • Build an error log
  4. Exam season

    Full papers and rest

    • Full timed papers
    • Short recall sessions each day
    • Sleep, food and breaks

How parents can help at home

Ask them to teach you

Explaining a topic out loud is one of the best ways to find the gaps.

Quiz with flashcards

Five minutes of questions over dinner beats an hour of re-reading.

Set up a timed paper

A quiet table, a timer and a mark scheme make practice feel like the real thing.

Thinking about medicine?

Medical schools look closely at GCSEs. KMMS, for example, asks for at least five GCSEs at grades 9 to 6, including English Language, Maths and the sciences. For many applicants, it also compares your results with your school’s.

Starting early makes the whole route easier. Read how KMMS contextual admissions work, or see our KMMS guide. At a Kent grammar school? Read medicine interview help for Kent grammar school students.
