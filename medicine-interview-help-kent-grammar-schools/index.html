Medicine interview help for Kent grammar school students

Medicine interview help for Kent grammar school students. At a selective school, strong grades are normal, so your experience, reflection and interview have to show what makes you different. Here is how to plan it.

Why grades alone will not set you apart

Many medicine applicants from grammar schools have excellent grades. Medical schools interview to choose between students who all look strong on paper.

That is why reflection and interview skills matter so much. They show how you communicate, how you think under pressure and whether your reasons for medicine are real.

KMMS says it looks for

resiliencerealistic commitment to Medicinecompassioncommunicationteamworkrespectunderstanding other viewpointsresponsibility

From Year 12 to an offer

  1. Year 12 autumn

    Start real experience

    • Begin weekly volunteering
    • Join or start a school medical society
    • Keep a short reflection diary
  2. Year 12 spring

    Research and prepare

    • Shortlist schools with our finder
    • Start reading around the NHS
    • Look at UCAT question types
  3. Year 12 summer

    UCAT and statement

    • UCAT registration opens around May
    • Sit the UCAT in the summer window
    • Draft your personal statement
  4. Year 13 autumn

    Apply

    • Finalise your four medical choices
    • Submit by 15 October at 6pm
    • Keep volunteering
  5. Year 13 winter

    Interviews

    • Practise MMI stations
    • Prepare examples for the NHS values
    • Practise group tasks

KMMS and your school’s results

Kent and Medway Medical School compares your grades with how students at your school usually perform. It calls this Contextualise Everyone.

At a school where very high grades are normal, very high grades are expected. KMMS may view a student who does well at a lower-performing school as doing more strongly relative to their school. It does not simply pick the highest grades or UCAT scores.

Make the most of a grammar school

Use your peers

  • Practise MMI stations in pairs
  • Run group tasks with four or five friends
  • Swap personal statement feedback

Stand out on reflection

  • Go deep on two or three experiences
  • Show what changed in how you act
  • Avoid the stories everyone tells

Choose schools wisely

  • Match your UCAT to how each school uses it
  • Check whether predicted grades are used
  • Compare them in our finder
